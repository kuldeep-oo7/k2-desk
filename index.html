Option premiums are from NSE’s 2026-10-22 settlement file (NSE posts the 2026-10-23 file after about 19:00 IST; the next build uses it); futures NOW is the 2026-10-23 last trade. The market was closed when this was built, so nothing here is executable right now. Premiums and the futures prices they are measured against both come from that same settlement file, so they describe the same moment. Check the price on your broker before placing anything. Net figures are after brokerage, STT at 0.15%, exchange, stamp and GST; the spread is extra and is only known where a live book was seen.
NO SHOCK SHIELD. Nothing on this page stops buys on a bad global morning — not a GIFT Nifty gap, a crude or S&P move, or an India VIX spike. Those signals were tested on NSE’s own data and failed (or had too few events to test), so they do not gate anything. Check the market yourself before acting on a shock day.
WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH. That any of it makes money. The paper ledger so far: futures 45 targets and 253 stops, 14.5% hit (95% range 7.5–21.4%); short futures 66 targets and 51 stops, 55.5% hit (95% range 33.7–77.2%); calls 339 targets and 1118 stops, 22.4% hit (95% range 15.6–29.2%); puts 1485 targets and 1013 stops, 58.2% hit (95% range 45.9–70.5%), over only 20 sessions of the same market. That says how this market went, not that a rule works — each row shows its own break-even. And every test fixed in advance so far, on data never used to build the rule, found no edge: the desk's own rating (replayed on six years of NSE data: −0.03% a trade on its buys, and its SELL/REDUCE shorts LOST −0.14% a trade, t = −3.3), 12-1 momentum, time-series momentum, low volatility, a 10-month market trend filter, and the NIFTY trend reader that now chooses between buys and puts (selling NIFTY in its downtrends: final year t = 0.21) — all six FAIL. The ranking is built on things that were measured, which is not the same as an edge that was proven. Size accordingly.
